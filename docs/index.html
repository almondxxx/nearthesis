How robust is the conclusion?

The premium recomputed under 2,000 random weightings. Pillar weights are drawn from a flat Dirichlet, and each metric's weight is scaled by 0.25 to 1.75×. If the sign holds across most draws, the reading doesn't depend on the weights chosen.

Does the premium predict returns?

Price change over the 90 days after each premium reading, grouped by bucket. The windows overlap and cover a single two-year regime. Read this as description, not a trading signal.

Method and sources

Scores. Each metric is smoothed with a 7-day mean (log scale for sizes). It is then ranked as a percentile against that chain's trailing 365 days. The rank uses past data only, so there is no look-ahead. Pillars are equal-weight means of the available metrics, and Fundamentals is the equal-weight mean of the pillars. Valuation is the same percentile applied to market cap.

Why percentiles against the chain's own history. NEAR and AVAX count accounts and fees differently. Comparing each chain with its own past avoids mixing units. The cross-chain comparison happens in the multiples instead: NEAR's multiple divided by AVAX's.

Definitions. Active accounts are daily distinct signers: NearBlocks for NEAR, activeSenders on the C-chain for AVAX. Returning accounts are active minus new. Revenue is fees burned: 70% of NEAR fees (the other 30% goes to contract developers) and 100% of AVAX C-chain fees. AVAX covers the C-chain only, not Avalanche L1s.

Sources (all public, no API keys). NEAR: NearBlocks and NEAR RPC for stake. AVAX: Avalanche Data API, CoinGecko for circulating supply, and DefiLlama for price. Both chains: DefiLlama for TVL, DEX volume, stablecoins, fees and revenue.

Known limits. NEAR stake history isn't available without an API key, so the pipeline saves one stake snapshot per run. NEAR's staking score starts once 180 snapshots exist; until then the NEAR tokenomics pillar uses supply growth and burn only. AVAX circulating supply before CoinGecko's 365-day window is extrapolated for market cap only, and supply-based AVAX metrics are left empty for that period. Neither chain has a protocol-level token buyback, so burn is the only supply sink measured.

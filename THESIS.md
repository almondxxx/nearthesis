# THESIS: Is NEAR Intents volume a better leading indicator of real network usage than NEAR's price?

**Snapshot:** weekly data from 2024-12-09 to 2026-11-12 (100 weeks). Every number below comes from the files
generated by `python run.py` at this snapshot: [`reports/thesis_results.md`](reports/thesis_results.md),
`docs/data/thesis.json` and `docs/data/correlation.json`. The live dashboard rebuilds daily, so its numbers will
move on from these:
- **Main page:** https://almondxxx.github.io/nearthesis/
- **Correlation page:** https://almondxxx.github.io/nearthesis/correlation.html

## The claim
> Cross-chain volume through NEAR Intents is a better **leading** indicator of real NEAR network usage than the
> NEAR token price.

It is tested as three questions:
1. Does Intents volume predict future network usage?
2. Is it a better predictor than NEAR's price?
3. Does Intents activity turn into economically valuable activity for NEAR?

## Short answer
**The claim is not supported as stated.** Intents volume tracks NEAR activity **in the same week**, and does so
better than price. But it does not reliably **lead** real usage, and on the measures that come closest to leading,
price does at least as well.

| Question | Full period (97 weekly changes) | Mature window, from 2025-04-17 (79) |
|---|---|---|
| Q1. Does Intents volume predict real usage? | **PARTLY**: forecasts improve by only about 1% (error ratio 0.99) and p = 0.38, so effectively no | **NO**: p = 0.93, error ratio 1.00 |
| Q2. Better predictor than price? | **MIXED**: neither is significant (Intents p = 0.40 and price p = 0.12, each tested with the other already in the model) | **MIXED, leaning to price**: price p = 0.06 with error ratio 0.99; Intents p = 0.93 |
| Q3. Does Intents activity create value for NEAR? | **PARTLY**: fees rise with Intents volume in the same week, but each dollar of volume earns NEAR less and less | **PARTLY** |
| Q1 on the broad Adoption Index (secondary target) | **NO**: p = 0.76 | **NO**: p = 0.69 |
| Q2 on the broad Adoption Index | **TIE**: neither predicts | **NO**: price adds information (p = 0.025) and Intents doesn't (p = 0.68) |

## Variables and data

| Role | Variable | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Candidate leading indicator | NEAR Intents volume (USD, daily, complete) | DefiLlama `summary/dexs/near-intents` |
| Robustness check for it | NEAR Intents successful swaps (1Click), estimated from 1,362 sampled hours (2 random hours per day, 2025-01-01 to 2026-11-12) | NEAR Intents Explorer API (JWT) |
| Competing indicator | NEAR price | NearBlocks |
| Market control | BTC price | DefiLlama coins API |
| **Primary target: Real Usage Index** | Geometric mean of **returning signers** (signed this week and in one of the previous 4 weeks) and **fees burned in NEAR**, two measures bots can't cheaply fake | BigQuery `sql/03_weekly_returning_signers.sql`; NearBlocks fees |
| Secondary target: broad Adoption Index | Active accounts, transactions, contracts or accounts receiving transactions | BigQuery `sql/01_daily_activity.sql`, `sql/02_contract_usage.sql` |
| Economic value (Q3) | Fees burned (NEAR and USD); fees per $1M of Intents volume | NearBlocks, DefiLlama |

The BigQuery dataset is current: its newest partition is 2026-11-13. BigQuery's active-account and transaction
counts match NearBlocks almost exactly in every quarter.

## Evidence FOR the claim
1. **Intents is the better *coincident* indicator.** In the same week, Intents volume growth correlates with:
   - Real Usage Index: **r = +0.29** (p = 0.003);
   - transactions: **+0.40** (p < 0.001);
   - fees burned: **+0.38** (p < 0.001);
   - new accounts: **+0.28** (p = 0.006).

   Price's same-week correlations with the same measures are +0.12, +0.06, +0.22 and +0.05; only the one with fees
   is significant.
2. **Intents slightly improves forecasts; price doesn't.** For the Real Usage Index over the full period, adding
   Intents gives an out-of-sample error ratio of 0.99, against 1.00 for price. The direction favours Intents, but
   the gain is about 1% and not significant (p = 0.38).
3. **Sampled swap counts show some positive leads:**
   - active accounts 1 week later (r = +0.24, p = 0.038, 77 weeks);
   - new accounts 8 weeks later (r = +0.25, p = 0.040, 70 weeks).

   In the week after the strongest quarter of swap weeks, active accounts changed **+1.8%**, against **−10.5%**
   after the weakest quarter (p = 0.025).
4. **Swap counts move with returning users.** The number of swaps (unaffected by crypto prices) moves with returning
   signers in the same week (**r = +0.26, p = 0.022**). USD volume doesn't (r = +0.06). Counting swaps rather than
   dollars brings Intents closer to real repeat usage.
5. **Intents activity does reach NEAR's fees** (Q3). In the same week, 10% more Intents volume goes with about
   **0.9% more fees in NEAR** (elasticity 0.092, 95% CI 0.049–0.135, p < 0.001, controlling for BTC). This holds
   in the mature window (0.084, p = 0.002).
6. **Intents is NEAR's one growing activity.** It moved $128.3K a day in its first 30 days on DefiLlama and
   $125.81M a day over the last 30, while the Real Usage Index fell from 100 to 12.6.

## Evidence AGAINST the claim
1. **There is no reliable lead.** Intents volume never shows a significant positive correlation with the Real
   Usage Index 1–8 weeks later. Q1's "PARTLY" rests on a 1% forecast gain with p = 0.38, and in the mature window
   it is a clear NO (p = 0.93).
2. **Price looks at least as good as a leading signal:**
   - price growth leads the Real Usage Index by 1 week (r = +0.23, p = 0.02);
   - price growth leads fees by 1 week (r = +0.25, p = 0.014);
   - in the mature window, price nearly passes the predictive test (p = 0.06, error ratio 0.99), and passes it for
     the broad Adoption Index (p = 0.025).

   Intents does neither.
3. **Real users don't move with Intents *volume*.** Returning signers show no link with Intents USD volume even in
   the same week (r = +0.06, p = 0.58), and neither do active accounts (r = −0.07). Swap counts do move with
   returning signers in the same week (point 4 above), but returning signers rise about **4 weeks before** swaps
   do (r = +0.24, p = 0.036): users come first, which is the opposite of the claim.
   - The reason shows in BigQuery: about **88K transactions a day go directly to `intents.near`, from only about
     166 signers** (30-day averages). Intents settlement runs through a small set of relayer and solver accounts,
     so cross-chain volume can grow without NEAR gaining users.
4. **Part of the same-week link is mechanical.** Intents' own settlement transactions are counted in NEAR's
   transactions and fees. Some of the +0.40 with transactions and +0.38 with fees is the same activity measured
   twice, not new adoption.
5. **Each dollar of Intents volume earns NEAR less.** NEAR fees per $1M of Intents volume, by quarter, from the first
   full quarter after Intents passed $1M a day:

   | Quarter | Q3 2025 | Q4 2025 | Q1 2026 | Q2 2026 | Q3 2026 | Q4 2026 to date |
   |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
   | Fees per $1M | $807 | $85 | $44 | $23 | $19 | $45 |

   That is **−98%** from Q3 2025 to Q3 2026, comparing complete quarters. Earlier quarters are Intents' launch
   period, when volume was tiny, so they aren't comparable. The pre-set Q3 rule, which compares against the first
   data quarter, gives the same verdict: PARTLY.
6. **The swap-count leads may be chance.** Across the 144 lead tests on the correlation page (3 predictors × 6
   metrics × 8 leads), only 3 positive leads are significant. At this significance level, chance alone would produce
   about 3.6. The swap counts also fail the stricter predictive test for the Real Usage Index (p = 0.88, error
   ratio 1.01).

## What would change my mind
- **Toward the claim:**
  - Intents volume or swap count passes the predictive test for the Real Usage Index (p < 0.05) **and** clearly
    improves out-of-sample forecasts (error ratio below about 0.95), in the mature window, as more weeks accumulate;
  - the swap-count leads survive the complete sample and a multiple-testing correction;
  - returning signers start rising with Intents volume, which would mean Intents brings repeat users rather than
    relayer traffic;
  - fees per $1M of Intents volume rise for several quarters.
- **Further against it:**
  - price's 1-week lead on the Real Usage Index and fees holds up as data grows;
  - removing `intents.near`'s own transactions and fees from the usage measures makes the same-week link disappear.

## What can't be computed from these sources
- **Intents volume outside 1Click from the Explorer API.** It only covers 1Click swaps, so total volume comes from
  DefiLlama.
- **Daily unique swappers.** Unique senders counted in sampled hours can't be scaled up to a full day.
- **Explorer swap counts before 2025-03-30.** Earlier sampled weeks contain no swaps.
- **Usage and fees excluding Intents' own settlement, at receipt level.** That needs
  `sql/optional/02b_contract_usage_receipts.sql`, which scans about 318 GB per 6 months (about 1.1 TB for the
  window), beyond BigQuery's free tier. It has not been run. Direct transactions to `intents.near` *are* counted
  from the cheaper transactions table.
- **Function-call-level contract usage.** The same cost applies. The cheaper proxy (accounts receiving
  transactions) is dominated by one automated sender until April 2026, so it is used only in the secondary index.
- **Cause and effect.** Every test here is correlational or predictive.

## Method, in brief
- **Weekly growth, not levels.** Complete Monday–Sunday weeks, as the week-on-week change in log level. In levels,
  Intents volume correlates **−0.85** with returning signers, because one grew while the other shrank. That's a
  trend effect, not a relationship.
- **Predictive test.** Each target is regressed on 2 weeks of its own history and 2 weeks of BTC, then 2 weeks of
  the predictor are added. An F-test (α = 0.05) checks whether they add information. Out of sample, next-week
  forecasts use an expanding window from mid-sample. "X beyond Y" adds X to a model that already contains Y.
- **The 2 April 2026 break.** NEAR transactions fell 72% and fees 62% in one day while active accounts barely
  moved, which is consistent with one large automated sender stopping. BigQuery confirms it is in the chain data.
  That week and the next get a dummy variable in every regression and are left out of the correlations.
- **Mature window.** It starts on the first day the 30-day average Intents volume passed $1M a day. Before that,
  Intents' weekly growth rates are launch noise.
- **Tests on made-up data.** The suite checks that the method finds a planted one-week lead and doesn't find one in
  pure noise (`tests/test_pipeline.py`).

## Changes made during the analysis
All of these were made in the open. The code history shows each one.
1. **"Active minus new accounts" was dropped** as a measure of returning users after it hit zero in some weeks:
   NearBlocks' new accounts can exceed active signers. It was replaced by BigQuery returning signers.
2. **The break dummy was widened** from one week to two after a −80% outlier appeared in the following week. A
   mid-week drop splits across two weekly averages.
3. **Only positive leads count as leads.** A significant *negative* one-week link (transactions and fees bouncing
   back) was first labelled "metric moves first". It is now reported separately as a bounce-back.
4. **A "NOT SUPPORTED" label was added** to tell "neither predicts" apart from "price predicts better".
5. **The primary target moved** from the broad Adoption Index to the Real Usage Index, because active accounts and
   transactions are heavily distorted by bots. The broad index is still reported, and the answer on it is
   unchanged: NO.

## Caveats
- About 100 weeks of noisy data can only detect a fairly strong lead, so "not supported" is not the same as "false".
- The sampled swap series has about 10% median weekly standard error (1,362 sampled hours, complete for
  2025-01-01 to 2026-11-12).
- This is research, not investment advice.
